Floor 1 · Remember · Guest

The Guest List

Every VIP has a team. Every team member is a verb. Tap each card to reveal the formula and an example.

💭 THINK LIKE A VIP FIRST

Shakira's car is dirty. What does she do?

Same event, different actor. have + thing + past participle = you arranged it and someone else did it.

Tu intuición ya conocía la regla.

Tip: vote by show of hands before tapping.

💡 EXPLAIN

The causative means the subject makes something happen through someone else. Your team has six members: have, get, make, let, help and the force / allow / require family.

Cada verbo es un miembro del equipo con un nivel de poder distinto.

🎬 EXAMPLE

✘ Shakira washed her car. She did it herself.
✔ Shakira had her car washed. The car-wash team did it, and she is the VIP.

🔍 WHY THIS VERB? WHY NOT THE OTHER?

Read each sentence and decide first. Then open it to check your reasoning.

You went to the barber and your hair is now short.

I had my hair cut.

✔ Yes. have + thing + participle: someone else cut it.

I got my hair cut.

✔ Yes. Same idea, more informal.

I made my hair cut.

✘ Not this one. make needs a person + base verb (made the barber cut). A thing can't be forced.

I let my hair cut.

✘ Not this one. let also needs a person, and a haircut is not a permission.

Your mom said: clean your room, no discussion.

My mom made me clean my room.

✔ Yes. make = no choice, and a base verb comes after me.

My mom got me clean my room.

✘ Not this one. get needs to (got me to clean) and means convincing.

My mom let me clean my room.

✘ Not this one. let = permission, the opposite of an order.

HAVE
have + person + base verb
have + thing + participle

I had the mechanic check the brakes. / I had my brakes checked.
GET
get + person + to + verb
get + thing + participle

I got my brother to help me. / I got my hair cut.
MAKE
make + person + base verb

My mom made me clean my room.
LET
let + person + base verb

She let me borrow her book.
HELP
help + person + (to) verb

He helped me (to) finish.
FORCE, ALLOW, REQUIRE
verb + person + to + verb

They forced me to sign the contract.

Golden rule

Person after the verb → a verb (base verb or to + verb)
Thing after the verb → past participle (the thing receives the action)

Persona → verbo. Cosa → participio pasado.

Floor 2 · Understand · Member

Who really does it?

💭 THINK LIKE A VIP FIRST

A pop star wants her assistant to bring coffee. It's a normal morning request. Which verb feels right?

The verb shows how much power the boss uses: let (permission) < have (normal request) < make (pressure).

El verbo revela cuánta presión usa el jefe.

Tip: vote by show of hands before tapping.

💡 EXPLAIN

The subject doesn't do the action: the subject causes it. That's the whole idea. The rest is choosing how much power you use.

En español: "Me corté el pelo" es ambiguo. En inglés debes elegir: cut (yo) o had cut (otro).

🎬 EXAMPLE

Same event, three powers:
My dad let me wash the car. (I wanted to)
My dad had me wash the car. (a normal request)
My dad made me wash the car. (no choice)

⚠️ NUANCE · THE SCALE HIDES THREE DIFFERENT IDEAS

  • Permission: let. The other person already wanted it.
  • Authority or service: have. You ask and it gets done.
  • Persuasion: get. You convince, with effort.
  • Pressure: make, force. There is no choice.

So let → force is a memory aid, not a ruler. Also, make can simply link a cause and a reaction: The movie made me cry (nobody is bossing anyone).

La escala mezcla permiso, autoridad, persuasión y presión.

🔍 WHY THIS VERB? WHY NOT THE OTHER?

Read each sentence and decide first. Then open it to check your reasoning.

You want to drive your dad's car.

My dad let me drive his car.

✔ Yes. let = he gave permission, and you wanted it.

My dad made me drive his car.

✘ Not this one. make = you had no choice. It only fits if you did not want to.

My dad had me drive his car.

✘ Not this one. have = he is the boss and requests it, like a work task. Odd for a teen.

I got my dad to let me drive.

≈ Possible, not the best. Correct, but it adds a step: you convinced him first.

The coach says everyone runs ten laps. Nobody wants to.

The coach made us run ten laps.

✔ Yes. make = obligation.

The coach let us run ten laps.

✘ Not this one. let = we asked for it.

The coach got us to run ten laps.

✘ Not this one. get = he convinced us with words. Here he ordered.

I cut my hair. → I did it (with scissors, in my bathroom).
I had my hair cut. → a stylist did it. I was the VIP.

The subject doesn't do the action: the subject causes it. That's the whole idea. The rest is choosing how much power you use.

En español: "Me corté el pelo" es ambiguo. En inglés debes elegir: cut (yo) o had cut (otro).

Power meter: how bossy are you?

have: Normal authority or a service. "I had my assistant send it."

VerbUse it when…Example
haveservices or delegating with normal authorityI had the mechanic check the brakes.
getconvincing, or informal talkI got my dad to lend me the car.

Tenses: where does it live?

Only have / get / make / let change tense. The participle or base verb never changes.

Very common: past simple (I had my hair cut yesterday), will / going to (I'll have it sent), present simple for habits (I have my car washed every Saturday), present continuous (I'm having my house painted), modals (You should have your eyes checked).
Rare: present perfect continuous, past perfect, future perfect. They are correct, but you'll almost never hear them.

Ojo: have y get causativos no tienen pasiva. Make sí: be made to + verbo.

"We had the plumber fix the sink." Who fixed it?

"He painted his room himself." Who painted?

Floor 3 · Apply · Manager

Give the orders

You're the manager now. Pick the correct word to complete each VIP order.

💭 THINK LIKE A VIP FIRST

A star talks about the same repair in two ways. Which pair sounds like real English?

A thing receives the action (repaired). A person does it (repair). Ask: who is the doer?

Cosa recibe → participio. Persona hace → verbo base.

Tip: vote by show of hands before tapping.

💡 EXPLAIN

Use the person-or-thing test:
Person → verb (have/make/let + base, get + to).
Thing → past participle.

Persona → verbo. Cosa → participio.

🎬 EXAMPLE

I had the mechanic check the brakes. (person)
I had my brakes checked. (thing)
I got my brother to help me. (person + to)

⚠️ NUANCE · HAVE NEEDS AUTHORITY OR A SERVICE

Between equals (siblings, friends), have + person sounds odd.
Odd: I had my sister bring me water. (you sound like her boss)
Natural: I got my sister to bring me water.
Works best with services (barber, mechanic, dentist) and real authority (manager, teacher).

Entre iguales, usa get + to o simplemente pide.

🔍 WHY THIS VERB? WHY NOT THE OTHER?

Read each sentence and decide first. Then open it to check your reasoning.

Your phone screen is broken and a technician fixes it.

I had my phone fixed.

✔ Yes. Service: someone else acts, participle after the thing.

I got my phone fixed.

✔ Yes. Same meaning, informal.

I made the technician fix my phone.

✘ Not this one. Correct form, but you sound rude: services are requested, not forced.

I fixed my phone.

✘ Not this one. This says you did it yourself.

Your brother did not want to help, but after a long talk he did.

I got my brother to help me.

✔ Yes. get + to = convincing with effort.

I made my brother help me.

✘ Not this one. make = force. You talked, you did not force him.

I had my brother help me.

≈ Possible, not the best. Odd between equals: have needs authority.

I'm going to ___ my teeth cleaned tomorrow.

The coach ___ us run ten laps. We didn't want to!

She ___ her brother to lend her the car. It took an hour!

My parents ___ me stay up late on Saturdays.

I had the mechanic ___ the brakes.

We got the house ___ before the party.

The boss forced me ___ the contract.

Floor 4 · Analyze · Executive

Error Hunters

Each sentence was "written by a VIP" and has a mistake. Find why it's wrong.

💭 THINK LIKE A VIP FIRST

A reporter writes: “The singer cut her hair at the salon.” Something feels off. What?

Analyzing means asking what story the grammar tells. Fix: The singer had her hair cut.

Un error de causativo es un error de “quién hizo qué”.

Tip: vote by show of hands before tapping.

💡 EXPLAIN

Four common errors: 1) to after make/let. 2) base verb after a thing. 3) no to after get + person. 4) doing it yourself when someone else did it.

Analizar es encontrar la regla que se rompió, no solo corregir.

🎬 EXAMPLE

✘ She made me to cry. → ✔ She made me cry.
✘ I had my car repair. → ✔ …repaired.
✘ I got him call. → ✔ …to call.

⚠️ NUANCE · NOT EVERY MAKE IS A COMMAND

Before you flag an error or a “bossy” sentence, check the cause: sometimes it is an emotion, and sometimes it is not even a person. Test yourself:

“The movie made me cry.” Is someone pressuring you?

“The rain made the game stop.” Who is the boss?

“The coach made us run ten laps.” Is someone pressuring you?

Make no siempre significa obligar.

🔍 WHY THIS VERB? WHY NOT THE OTHER?

Read each sentence and decide first. Then open it to check your reasoning.

Find the type of mistake: form or meaning?

She made me to cry.

✘ Not this one. Form error: make takes a base verb, no to.

I had my car repair.

✘ Not this one. Form error: a thing needs the participle (repaired).

I made the mechanic fix my car.

✘ Not this one. Meaning error: the form is right, but it sounds like you forced a service. Use had.

I cut my hair at the salon.

✘ Not this one. Meaning error: it tells the wrong story, because you did not cut it.

I had my car repaired.

✔ Yes. Right form and right story.

Patterns to compare

had the mechanic check ✔ / had the mechanic checked ✘
got the mechanic to check ✔ / got the mechanic check ✘
I had my car repaired = someone repaired it. I had repaired my car = past perfect, I did it.
Questions: Did you have your car repaired? ✔ / Have you your car repaired? ✘
Careful: He had his wallet stolen is bad luck, not a request. Context decides.

✘ She made me to cry. Why is it wrong?

✘ I had my car repair. Why is it wrong?

✘ I got my brother help me. Why is it wrong?

✘ I cut my hair at the salon. Why does it sound odd?

Floor 5 · Evaluate · Director

The Judge

Several sentences may be grammatical, but only one fits the situation best. Judge like a director.

💭 THINK LIKE A VIP FIRST

A star describes her barber two ways. Which sentence makes her sound like a diva?

Both are grammatical. The verb you choose shows your relationship with the other person.

Evaluar es decidir cuál conviene, no solo cuál es correcta.

Tip: vote by show of hands before tapping.

💡 EXPLAIN

Evaluating means choosing the best sentence, not just a correct one. Use four criteria: who acts, how much pressure, how polite, how formal.

Varias oraciones pueden estar bien; solo una encaja mejor con la situación.

🎬 EXAMPLE

I made the barber cut my hair. → correct but rude.
I had my hair cut. → natural.
I got the barber to cut it shorter. → I convinced him.

⚠️ NUANCE · WHERE THE SCALE BREAKS

Four nuances to judge with: 1) the scale mixes permission, persuasion and pressure. 2) have needs authority or a service. 3) make can be cause and effect. 4) the subject can be a thing (The rain made the game stop).

Between two friends, which is more natural?

Which sentence uses make with NO pressure?

Which verb on the scale is about persuasion, not authority?

Un buen juez sabe cuándo la regla no alcanza.

🔍 WHY THIS VERB? WHY NOT THE OTHER?

Read each sentence and decide first. Then open it to check your reasoning.

A student stays after class. The teacher decided, not the student.

The teacher made us stay after class.

✔ Yes. Faithful to the situation: no choice.

The teacher had us stay after class.

≈ Possible, not the best. Acceptable, but softer: it hides the pressure.

The teacher let us stay after class.

✘ Not this one. let = we wanted it. That changes the facts.

You convinced your grandpa (after a long talk) to lend you his guitar. Best sentence?

You went to a barber. Which is the most natural and polite?

A sad song brought tears to your eyes. Best sentence?

Your teacher gave you permission to leave early. Best sentence?

Floor 6 · Create · The Boss

Your VIP Day

Choose a VIP and write their day. Use at least five causatives: one each of have, get, make, let and one service (have/get + thing + participle).

💭 THINK LIKE A VIP FIRST

Your VIP wakes up. Which job would they NEVER do alone? Pick one.

Every job a VIP doesn't do alone is a causative waiting to be written. Which power will you choose?

Cada tarea delegada es una oración causativa.

Tip: vote by show of hands before tapping.

💡 EXPLAIN

Creating means combining everything. Your VIP recipe has five ingredients: a service (have/get + thing + participle), have + person, get + person + to, make, let.

Cinco ingredientes = un día VIP completo.

🎬 EXAMPLE

At 7 a.m., the singer had her assistant bring coffee. Then she got her hair styled. Her manager made her rehearse, but the band let her choose the last song.

⚠️ NUANCE · BEFORE YOU WRITE: THREE POWER TIPS

  • Use make twice: once for pressure (The coach made us run) and once for a reaction (The song made her cry).
  • Use have for services and authority, and get when you convince an equal.
  • Try one cause that is not a person: The storm made the concert stop.

Estos matices hacen que tu VIP suene natural.
